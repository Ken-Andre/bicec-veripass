## What they really sell

1. Credibility before product: regulated group, clients, revenue, enterprise trust.
2. Category framing: Digital-ID + Digital-KYC + Client Lifecycle Management.
3. Scope: global document coverage, all counterparties, omni-channel reach.
4. Architecture: central KYC/AML file repository, module factory, workflow engine, compliance portal.
5. Lifecycle: collection, profiling, acceptance logic, monitoring, remediation.
6. Module depth: ID capture, liveness, screening, document management, acceptance logic, alerts, risk propagation.
7. Use cases: finance and non-finance sectors to prove market applicability.
8. Benefits: conversion, productivity, security, data quality.
9. Next step: workshop / PoC / scale.

## What VeriPass should copy as logic, not claims

- Copy the category move: from app to KYC lifecycle rail.
- Copy the modular map: show modules as business capabilities, not features.
- Copy the central repository idea: one dossier, many roles, one audit trail.
- Copy the lifecycle arc: onboarding today, monitoring/remediation as extension path.
- Copy the PoC discipline: measure locally before buying or scaling.

## What VeriPass must not copy

- No global ID coverage claim.
- No KYB/legal entity readiness claim.
- No integration with CRM/core as current fact.
- No automated acceptance as current target.
- No named providers such as Worldcheck/Factiva unless BICEC procures them.
- No huge client logos or scale claims.

## Adapted BICEC message

The global market has already packaged digital KYC as lifecycle infrastructure. VeriPass gives BICEC a local, sovereign first rail for the same strategic territory: mobile capture, evidence repository, human validation, AML blocking, audit, and pilot metrics. The decision is not to buy a foreign platform now. The decision is to measure BICEC's own rail before the market forces that purchase from outside.
