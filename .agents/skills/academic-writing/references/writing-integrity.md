# Writing Integrity And Authorial Voice

Use this reference when the user mentions AI-detector flags, false positives, "humanizing" text, authorial voice, style calibration, permitted AI assistance, or evidence of original work.

## Boundary

Do:
- Improve academic clarity, precision, voice consistency, and evidence trail.
- Help the user comply with course, journal, supervisor, or institutional AI-use policy.
- Preserve the user's genuine claims, drafts, notes, and style.
- Explain why detector scores should be treated cautiously.

Do not:
- Promise that text will pass an AI detector.
- Rewrite solely to reduce a detector score.
- Add artificial mistakes, slang, typos, or random variation to disguise AI assistance.
- Fabricate draft history, notes, citations, sources, or authorship claims.
- Remove required AI-use disclosure.

## Detector Reality

Treat detector outputs as weak signals, not proof. OpenAI discontinued its own text classifier in July 2023 because of low accuracy and warned that classifiers should not be primary decision tools. Independent academic work continues to find context sensitivity, false-positive risk, and weaker performance on scientific or EFL writing.

Useful sources:
- OpenAI classifier limitations: https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/
- Liang et al., bias against non-native English writers: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819
- Weber-Wulff et al., detector reliability testing: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15666
- Recent academic-context reliability study: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40979-026-00213-1
- Turnitin false-positive guidance hub: https://www.turnitin.com/solutions/topics/ai-writing/

## Safe Intake

Ask for only the missing items needed for the task:
- Target policy: course/journal/conference/institution AI rules.
- Target audience and venue.
- The user's draft, outline, notes, source annotations, and data summaries.
- Prior writing samples, if they want style calibration.
- Whether AI assistance must be disclosed and what form the disclosure should take.

If the user provides fewer than three prior samples, build only a cautious partial style profile.

## Style Calibration

Use only writing the user authored. Do not learn from co-authored sections unless the user identifies their own parts.

Extract these dimensions:
1. Sentence rhythm: average sentence length, variation, short-sentence usage, long explanatory sentence patterns.
2. Paragraph rhythm: average sentences per paragraph, section-specific variation, use of short emphasis paragraphs.
3. Vocabulary preferences: hedging verbs, transition words, reporting verbs, level of formality.
4. Citation integration: narrative vs parenthetical citations, citation density, evidence placement.
5. Modifier style: lean vs elaborate prose, concrete vs abstract framing.
6. Register shifts: how Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, and Abstract differ.

Priority hierarchy:
1. Discipline conventions are hard constraints.
2. Target journal or assignment conventions are strong constraints.
3. Personal style is a soft guide where it does not conflict with the first two.

When style conflicts with a norm, follow the norm and briefly note the conflict once.

## Scientific Clarity Audit

Apply these passes before any detector-related discussion:

### 1. Clutter
Cut dead-weight phrases such as "due to the fact that", "in order to", "it is worth noting that", "in terms of", and "at the present time". Replace inflated phrases with direct wording.

### 2. Voice And Verbs
Prefer clear actors and strong verbs. Convert passive voice only when it hides responsibility or makes the sentence harder to follow. Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or required by disciplinary convention.

### 3. Sentence Architecture
Flag buried predicates, overloaded sentences, run-ons, and monotonous sentence length. Vary rhythm because it improves readability. Do not create fake variation.

### 4. Terminology
Keep technical terms consistent. Do not cycle synonyms for the same concept to avoid repetition; repetition of defined terms is often clearer.

### 5. Numbers And Citations
Check that sample sizes, percentages, table values, figure values, units, significant figures, and cited claims agree across the manuscript. Prefer primary sources for factual or numerical claims.

## Formulaic Prose Review

Flag these as writing-quality issues, not detector-evasion targets:
- Overused generic verbs: leverage, delve, showcase, navigate, underscore, foster.
- Inflated adjectives: crucial, pivotal, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, robust when vague, comprehensive when unjustified.
- Vague abstractions: landscape, realm, tapestry, multifaceted, nuanced without specifics.
- Throat-clearing openers: "It is important to note that", "This section will discuss", "When it comes to", "In today's rapidly evolving".
- Template patterns: every paragraph same length, every argument split into exactly three points, repeated "Moreover/Furthermore/Additionally" chains, repeated "not X but Y" structures.

Fix by choosing precise terms, removing filler, and letting the structure follow the evidence.

## Process Evidence

When the user wants protection against false accusations, help assemble a truthful evidence pack:
- Timeline of drafts, notes, commits, comments, or tracked changes.
- Source list and annotations showing how claims were developed.
- Summary of AI assistance used, if any, aligned with the relevant policy.
- Before/after edit summary explaining that edits improved clarity without changing claims.
- Remaining author decisions: claims, interpretation, citation choices, and final approval.

Do not fabricate any item. If evidence is missing, say so.

## If A Detector Flags The Work

Help the user respond calmly:
1. Do not treat the score as proof.
2. Gather drafts, notes, sources, version history, and assignment/journal policy.
3. Identify any permitted AI assistance and prepare a truthful disclosure if required.
4. Offer a writing-quality review that focuses on clarity and voice, not score manipulation.
5. Draft a concise explanation of process and evidence for an instructor, supervisor, editor, or integrity office.
